## What client gallery software actually needs to do in 2026

A client gallery platform is where your finished photos go to be delivered, viewed, downloaded, and often bought. The bar has risen past simple photo hosting. The feature set serious photographers now expect includes:

Password or email-gated access with a client-branded landing page. Favouriting and light proofing so clients can mark their picks. Download options with watermarked previews and full-resolution tiers. A print store so clients can order without leaving the gallery. And a fast, clean experience on mobile, because that's where most clients open their photos.

The platforms below all do the core job. Where they differ is *emphasis*, some prioritize gallery beauty, some business tools, some price, some storage. Here's how to match one to how you actually work.

## 1. Pixieset - best all-in-one

Pixieset is the default choice for most wedding and family photographers, and it earns that spot by doing most things well at a fair price. Paid plans start from $8/month (billed annually) or $10/month monthly for 10GB storage, with a limited free tier (3GB, with 15% commission on sales at the free level). Costs rise with storage, and RAW support arrives on its Pro plan ($24/mo annual / $30/mo monthly).

The honest limits: its CRM is lighter than ShootProof's, and its gallery customization is a step below Pic-Time's. But for sheer "does everything competently in one place," Pixieset is hard to beat.

## The honest trade-offs

A few things the marketing pages tend to skip:

**Free plans are for delivery, not selling.** Most free tiers cap storage and attach a sales commission (Pixieset takes 15% on its free tier, for example). They're fine to test or for occasional low-volume delivery, not for running a print business.

**Pricing scales with storage.** Every platform here charges more as you store more. Compare at *your* storage level, not the headline rate, a busy wedding shooter pays well above the entry price.

**RAW support is limited.** If you proof on RAW files before editing, most of these are JPEG-only. SmugMug supports RAW on every plan; Pixieset only from its Pro tier; CloudSpot on full-suite plans.

**These don't cull or edit.** Gallery platforms deliver and sell finished photos. Sorting and editing happen earlier, in separate tools.

## FAQ

**What is the best client gallery software in 2026?**
For most photographers, Pixieset, it balances polished galleries, a website builder, and a strong print store at a fair price. ShootProof is the best value with business tools included, and Pic-Time leads on premium design and marketing automation.

**What is the cheapest client gallery software?**
CloudSpot has the cheapest full-suite entry ($12/mo annual [$144/yr]) and a usable free plan; Zenfolio starts at $7/month (Basic); ShootProof starts at $8.33/month (annual). All scale with storage, and free tiers usually attach a sales commission.

**Which client gallery has the best print store?**
Pixieset's print store is widely considered best-in-class. ShootProof's store is commission-free (you keep more profit), and Pic-Time's automated marketing is designed to drive the most print sales over time.

**Which gallery software includes a website builder?**
Pixieset, ShootProof, SmugMug, and Zenfolio include website builders. Pic-Time does not, so you'd host your portfolio site elsewhere if you choose it.

**Do client gallery platforms cull or edit photos?**
No. They deliver, host, and sell finished photos. Culling and editing are handled by separate tools before you upload your gallery.
