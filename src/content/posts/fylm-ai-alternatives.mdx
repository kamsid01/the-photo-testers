## What Fylm.ai is, and why photographers use it

Fylm.ai is a cloud-based colour grading platform for creating film-style looks and LUTs. You work in a browser with no software to install, and it includes AI features such as AI Color Extract and its NeuralFilmAI engine.

It is used by both videographers and photographers. The photography angle is specific: Fylm.ai can export XMP profiles for Lightroom and Camera Raw, and ICC profiles for Capture One, so you can build a look once and apply it to your stills in the editor you already use. It runs on a freemium model, a free tier with limits on resolution, export formats and storage, and a paid subscription that unlocks the advanced AI features and higher-resolution exports. Fylm.ai does not publish detailed prices on third-party pages, so check the current plans on its own site.

## Why look for a Fylm.ai alternative

A few honest reasons photographers go looking:

**It leans toward video.** Fylm.ai and most tools in this space are built primarily for film and video colour grading. If you only grade stills, some of the feature set is not for you.

**You want grading inside your editor.** Fylm.ai is a separate cloud app. Some photographers would rather grade with a plugin that lives inside Premiere, Final Cut, DaVinci Resolve, or their photo editor.

**You want something simpler, or free.** If your real need is "make my photos' colour consistent" rather than building cinematic LUTs, there are lighter and cheaper options.

Here is what to consider instead, and who each suits.

### Colourlab AI
AI colour matching and balancing that runs as a plugin for Premiere Pro, Final Cut Pro and DaVinci Resolve. If you want grading to happen inside the editor you already work in rather than a separate cloud app, this is the closest fit, though it is aimed mainly at video work.

### Color.io
Cloud-based AI grading, the most direct like-for-like with Fylm.ai. It adapts pre-trained cinematic LUTs to your specific lighting and camera settings, and works for both stills and video. Worth a look if you like Fylm.ai's browser-based approach but want to compare the look and the workflow.

### Polarr (AI Color Match)
The most photography-friendly option here. Polarr applies the colour and tone of a reference image to your photo in one click. If your goal is simply "make this shot match that look" rather than building and exporting LUTs, this is far simpler than a full grading platform.

### LUTGen.ai
Generates LUTs with AI, often from a text prompt or a reference image. Useful if what you actually want from Fylm.ai is the LUT output, and you would rather generate looks quickly than grade them by hand.

### Media.io (Auto Color Correct)
A free, one-click online auto-correction tool for photos and videos. Not a serious grading platform, but if you just need a fast, free colour fix now and then, it does the job without a subscription.

### Lightroom / Capture One (the one you may already have)
Worth saying plainly: if you are a photographer whose real need is consistent, attractive colour, your editor already has colour grading built in. Lightroom's Color Grading panel and Capture One's colour tools cover a lot of what photographers reach for Fylm.ai to do. Before paying for another tool, it is worth checking whether what you already own gets you there.

## An honest word on fit

Most of these tools, Fylm.ai included, come from the video and film colour grading world. They are genuinely useful for photographers who want cinematic, LUT-driven looks and the ability to build a profile once and reuse it. But if you are a wedding or portrait photographer whose colour mostly happens in Lightroom, a dedicated grading platform may be more than you need. Match the tool to whether you are chasing a specific cinematic look (worth a grading tool) or just consistent, clean colour (your editor probably covers it).

## FAQ

**What is Fylm.ai used for?**
Fylm.ai is a cloud-based colour grading platform for creating film-style looks and LUTs. It works for both stills and video, and can export XMP profiles for Lightroom and Camera Raw and ICC profiles for Capture One, which is why photographers use it.

**Is there a free alternative to Fylm.ai?**
Media.io offers free one-click auto colour correction, and several tools (including Fylm.ai itself) have free tiers with limits. For photographers, your existing editor's colour grading is effectively a "free" alternative you already own.

**What is the closest alternative to Fylm.ai?**
Color.io is the most direct like-for-like, cloud-based AI grading that adapts cinematic LUTs and works for both stills and video. Colourlab AI is the closest if you want a plugin inside your editor instead of a cloud app.

**Do photographers actually need a colour grading tool?**
Not necessarily. If you want cinematic, LUT-driven looks or to build a profile once and reuse it, a grading tool helps. If you just want consistent, clean colour, Lightroom's or Capture One's built-in tools usually cover it.
