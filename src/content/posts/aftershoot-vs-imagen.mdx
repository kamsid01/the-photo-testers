# Aftershoot vs Imagen: Which AI Editor Wins in 2026?

By The Photo Testers team · Published July 2026 · Updated October 2026 · 9 min read

*We evaluate tools through hands-on testing and structured research. Some links below are affiliate links, which may earn a commission if you subscribe. Pricing, plans, and technical specifications verified October 2026 against official vendor documentation.*

<QuickAnswer>

**Short answer:** Aftershoot is best for high-volume photographers and those who need to work offline, with flat annual pricing covering culling, editing, and retouching on your local computer. Imagen is best for Lightroom Classic users seeking precise style matching and nuanced skin tones, offered via Pay-As-You-Go ($0.05/photo + add-ons) or a flat Limitless subscription for eligible solo shooters.

**The key tradeoff:** Aftershoot runs 100% offline on your own GPU with predictable costs. Imagen processes in the cloud and delivers superior style adaptation, but requires active internet and charges per photo for multi-shooter studios.

## One thing to know before you read any comparison

Search "Aftershoot vs Imagen" and most of what comes back is published by Aftershoot or Imagen.

Imagen's blog concludes Imagen is the superior choice. Aftershoot's blog concludes Aftershoot handles more of your workflow. Both are well written. Both are marketing.

The accuracy numbers show the problem clearly. Aftershoot reports a 90 percent accuracy rate in spotting technical flaws. Imagen reports 99.2 percent for detecting things like closed eyes and soft focus. Those are self-reported figures, measured different ways on different image sets. You cannot compare them directly, and neither company is going to publish a number that makes it look worse.

So treat the vendor stats as marketing and judge these tools on what you can actually verify: what they cost at your volume, where they run, and what they do to your workflow. That is what the rest of this covers.

## The cost math nobody runs for you

This is the decision for most photographers, so let us do it properly.

**Imagen offers two distinct pricing models:** Pay-as-you-go (PAYG) and Limitless. On PAYG, base editing costs $0.05 per photo (subject to a $7 monthly minimum spend). Optional AI tools add $0.01 per photo each: Crop ($0.01), Straighten ($0.01), Subject Mask ($0.01), and Smooth Skin ($0.01). With all four optional tools enabled, editing rises to $0.09 per photo—an 80% increase over the base editing fee. For eligible solo photographers who shoot their own work, Imagen also offers a flat Limitless subscription ($129/month billed annually ($1,548/year) or $165/month billed monthly). Crucially, Limitless is restricted to individual photographers shooting their own jobs; multi-shooter studios, associate teams, and post-production houses must use PAYG or pooled Business plans.

**Aftershoot uses a modular flat-fee subscription.** Select (AI culling only) is $120/year billed annually ($10/month equivalent) or $15/month billed monthly. Edit (AI editing with personal or marketplace profiles) is $360/year billed annually ($30/month equivalent) or $25/month billed monthly. Complete (bundling culling, editing, and portrait retouching) is $540/year billed annually ($45/month equivalent) or $49/month billed monthly. Volume never increases your price, and all processing runs locally on your computer.

**Now compare real workflow numbers based on delivered edits.** A common misconception is pricing an entire RAW shoot as though every frame is edited. In reality, a wedding producing 2,500 RAW images is typically culled down to approximately 600 to 800 delivered photos, and only those selected frames are edited. Consider a wedding photographer shooting 20 weddings a year and delivering 700 edited photos per wedding (14,000 edited photos annually):

- **On Imagen PAYG:** 14,000 photos at $0.05 base editing equals $700/year. Adding AI crop and straighten ($0.07/photo) brings the total to $980/year. With all four AI add-ons ($0.09/photo), it reaches $1,260/year.
- **On Imagen Limitless:** If eligible as a solo photographer, the flat subscription costs $129/month billed annually ($1,548/year) or $165/month billed monthly.
- **On Aftershoot Edit:** The flat annual plan is $360/year ($30/month equivalent), or $540/year for Aftershoot Complete (including culling and retouching).
- In this 14,000-image wedding workflow, Aftershoot Edit ($360/year) saves $340 to $900/year compared to Imagen PAYG ($700 to $1,260/year), and saves $1,188/year compared to Imagen Limitless ($1,548/year).

**At lower volume, however, the math flips.** If you shoot portrait or family sessions delivering 2,000 photos across an entire year, Imagen PAYG base editing costs $100/year (or $84/year at the $7/month minimum). In that low-volume scenario, Imagen PAYG is less than half the cost of Aftershoot Edit ($360/year), making pay-as-you-go genuinely economical for part-time, seasonal, or portrait-focused photographers.

**The studio boundary case:** For studios shooting 50 weddings with associate photographers (35,000 delivered edits annually), Imagen Limitless is not available. At $0.05/photo base, Imagen PAYG costs $1,750/year (or $2,450/year with crop and straighten). Here, Aftershoot’s flat workstation license ($360/year for Edit, $540/year for Complete) delivers significant cost savings.

## Culling: Aftershoot's home turf

Aftershoot built its reputation here. You import a shoot and it analyses sharpness, expressions, open eyes, duplicates, pose variations, and overall quality, then makes the selections for you. It is the more automated of the two, cutting manual selection time significantly.

Imagen culls too, but it is a secondary feature rather than the main event. It does have one genuinely clever twist: it uses a "cull in" approach that focuses on picking keepers, and it can show you edited previews while you are still culling. Seeing roughly what the final edit will look like while choosing keepers is useful, and no other tool really does it.

The honest caveat on both: neither is hands-off. Photographers consistently report needing a review pass, particularly in low light and fast-moving scenes. Budget for that regardless of which you pick.

## Editing: Imagen's home turf

Imagen is the stronger editor, and its reputation for skin tones on weddings and portraits is well earned. It delivers clean, consistent edits directly inside Lightroom Classic.

Its edge comes from two things. First, personal profiles: Imagen's personal AI profile learns from over 2,000 of your edited photos to replicate your style. Second, and this matters if you are newer, it offers Talent profiles built by well-known photographers, so you can get a strong look immediately without a big back catalogue of your own edits.

Aftershoot's editing is capable but asks more of you. Its personal profiles want 2,500 or more of your previously edited RAW files, with 5,000 plus recommended for best accuracy. If you do not have thousands of consistently edited photos already, you are leaning on its marketplace styles rather than a profile trained on you.

**So if you are early in your career, Imagen's borrowed-style approach gets you a good result faster.** If you have years of consistent edits behind you, either tool can learn from them.

## Where they run, and why it matters

This is the difference people underrate until it bites them.

**Aftershoot runs locally.** Your images never leave your computer, you can work on a plane or at a venue with bad wifi, and there is no upload wait. The trade-off is that your machine does the work, and Aftershoot wants 16GB of RAM as a minimum.

**Imagen processes in the cloud.** That makes it far lighter on your computer, it runs fine on 8GB of RAM, but it needs a stable, fast internet connection. On an older laptop that is an advantage. On a slow connection with a large wedding catalog, it is a real bottleneck.

One more workflow point. Aftershoot exports to Lightroom Classic, Capture One, Photoshop, or straight to a folder. Imagen is built around Lightroom Classic.

## What each one misses

**Aftershoot's weak spots.** Editing profiles need a large back catalogue to shine. Culling needs human review in difficult lighting and candid motion. Complete suite carries a higher upfront cost ($480/yr).

**Imagen’s weak spots.** Pay-as-you-go pricing scales directly with volume, and Limitless is restricted to solo shooters editing their own work. Cloud processing requires an active internet connection and substantial upload bandwidth for large catalogs. Culling is a secondary feature rather than a dedicated tool. Finally, optional AI add-ons ($0.01 each for crop, straighten, subject mask, and smooth skin) increase base editing from $0.05 to $0.09 per photo.

Neither replaces your judgment. The realistic gain from either is time, not a hands-off pipeline.

## So, which one?

**Choose Aftershoot if** you shoot weddings or events at volume, you want predictable costs, you work where the internet is unreliable, or you want culling, editing, and retouching in one place.

**Choose Imagen if** you edit in Lightroom Classic, your volume is moderate or seasonal, editing quality and style matching matter more to you than culling, or you are newer and want to borrow a professional look immediately.

**A lot of photographers use both.** Cull in Aftershoot, edit in Imagen. It costs more, but it puts each tool on the job it is actually best at.

Both let you try before paying. Aftershoot offers a 30-day free trial with full access and no credit card required. Imagen offers 1,000 free AI edits.

## FAQ

**Is Aftershoot or Imagen cheaper?**
It depends on your volume and business structure. Aftershoot charges a flat fee ($240/yr for Edit, $480/yr for Complete) with unlimited local processing, making it significantly cheaper at high wedding volume. Imagen charges $0.05 per photo on pay-as-you-go (winning at low portrait volume under 4,000 photos/year), but also offers a flat Limitless subscription ($129/mo annual [$1,548/yr]) for eligible solo photographers.

**Which has better AI culling?**
Aftershoot, and it is the tool's core strength. Imagen culls too, and its ability to preview edits while culling is genuinely useful, but culling is not its main focus. Both require human review on complex receptions and candid movement.

**Which has better AI editing?**
Imagen generally, particularly for skin tones in weddings and portraits, and its Talent profiles let newer photographers borrow a professional look immediately. Aftershoot's editing is solid but its personal profiles need thousands of your past edits to shine.

**Does Aftershoot work offline?**
Yes. Aftershoot processes locally on your computer, so no internet is needed and your files never leave your machine. Imagen requires an internet connection because it processes in the cloud.

**Can I use both together?**
Yes, and many photographers do. A common setup is culling in Aftershoot and editing in Imagen. You pay for both, but each handles the job it is strongest at.

**Do these tools work with Capture One?**
Aftershoot exports to Capture One. Imagen is built around Lightroom Classic.
